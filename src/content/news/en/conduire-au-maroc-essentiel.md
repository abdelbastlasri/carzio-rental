Driving in Morocco is accessible if you know what to expect. Main roads are generally in good condition, cities are dense, and local habits sometimes differ from yours. Here is the essential to go prepared.

## The basic rules

- Drive on the **right**, like in continental Europe.
- **Roundabouts** are common in towns: priority is on the right, as in France.
- **Speed limits** depend on the zone: roughly **60 in built-up areas, 100 on secondary roads, 120 on motorways**. Posted signs take precedence over our examples.
- Using a **phone** while driving is punishable if spotted; use a mount or hands-free kit.
- **Seatbelts** are mandatory for all front passengers.

## Roads day to day

National roads between Agadir and Marrakech (N8) or Essaouira (N1) are usually passable, sometimes winding. In the mountains the weather changes quickly: stay alert on rainy days. Avoid night driving outside built-up areas.

## Fuel

**Diesel** is the most common fuel in Morocco. Petrol stations are frequent on main roads. Carzio's fuel policy is simple: you return the car at the **same level** you received it. Fill up just before returning to avoid refuelling charges.

## Parking

- In towns, free spaces exist but are scarce in the evening.
- Near beaches or hotels, prefer guarded car parks: a few dirhams protect the car and its contents.
- Never leave valuables visible (bag, camera, laptop): it is the first target of opportunistic theft.

## Police checkpoints

Checks are frequent, especially on interurban roads. Slow down, show your licence, the vehicle registration and your rental contract. Stay polite and calm: interactions are generally cordial when you are in order.

## Signage

Signs are bilingual Arabic/French, sometimes symbolic. When in doubt, trust signs and road markings, or ask. Your contract includes Carzio roadside assistance: we answer in minutes if you are unsure.

## Situations to avoid

- Unpaved roads at night: avoid them or take them by day only.
- Dense urban zones at rush hour (Agadir around 8–9 am and 6–7.30 pm).
- Mountain roads in the rain: they demand more attention and more distance.
